Outreach

Every message represents CodePath's brand and mission. Keep it concise, human, and inspired.

Our tone

  • Warmth and humanity: personable, never sterile and corporate
  • Ambition and urgency: show we are doing something big
  • Clarity and simplicity: write like you speak, and avoid jargon
  • Credibility and pride: show impact without exaggeration

Core style principles

ElementHow to apply it
VoiceProfessional, upbeat, inclusive, conversational
Length100 to 150 words for first emails, 50 to 80 for follow-ups. If it runs longer than a phone screen intro, it is too long.
ToneMission-driven, human, and clear
Perspective70% candidate, 30% CodePath. Lead with why they would care and what they would own.
PersonalizationOne personal detail plus one mission tie
FormattingShort paragraphs, one or two links, a clear call to action

Do and don't

Do

  • Use Claude Cowork plugins to align language with CodePath branding
  • Check the Career Page for branding cues
  • Link the 2025 Annual Report to show how we differ from other tech startups
  • Use the Talking Points for External Conversations (March 2025) for terminology
  • Open with a short, warm line that feels written for them
  • Use AI for a first draft, then add your edits
  • Use "we" more than "I"
  • Mention backers (Google, Salesforce, Blue Meridian) sparingly
  • Use only one or two links (Intro Deck, Annual Report, Greenhouse link)
  • Close with a human call to action, like "Would you be open to a quick chat?"

Avoid

  • Sending AI-generated outreach without a human review
  • Overusing buzzwords such as "innovative," "world-class," "disruptive," or "sits at the intersection of"
  • Leading with compensation or benefits
  • Pasting in the full mission statement or generic paragraphs
  • Using "diverse" or "diversity." Use phrases like "advancing access" or "removing barriers."
  • Excessive emojis or exclamation marks
  • Em dashes

Pre-send checklist

  • Subject line under 8 words
  • First line personalized
  • Three short paragraphs at most
  • Mission tie-in present
  • One or two links, no attachments
  • Warm, authentic close or call to action

What the data says

These figures come from Gem's analysis of more than 4 million outreach sequences. Generic recruiting emails no longer work: 76.6% of outreach gets opened and only 22.6% gets replies.

  • Timing: best send times are 8 am (68.0% open rate), 4 pm (67.3%), and 10 am (67.0%).
  • Weekends: few recruiters send on weekends, and weekend messages open at 66% or higher. For technical roles, 3 of the top 4 send times fall on weekends. For non-tech roles, stick to business hours.
  • Length: 101 to 150 words for first messages.
  • Personalization: highly personalized messages see a 73% engagement rate.
  • Subject lines: 3 to 9 words works best. Include company names and job titles.
  • Sequences: five follow-ups bring 2x more replies and nearly 68% higher "interested" rates than one-off emails. After stage 5, engagement flattens.
  • Leadership: having the hiring manager or an executive send one follow-up lifts reply rates by over 50%, yet only 22% of recruiters do it.

Benchmarks to track: open rates above 77% and reply rates above 23%.

CodePath tips

  • If short messages are not working, experiment with length and style. Play up the vision, link media, and name-drop leadership with LinkedIn hyperlinks.
  • We tend to cap sequences at 3 messages and use other touchpoints after that: LinkedIn connection requests, comments, shares, and reactions on prospects' posts. If Juicebox does not surface an email, check the prospect's website.
  • Connection requests often get quick responses when emails are ignored.
  • Alias emails: work with Jones IT to set up an alias for a hiring manager's email. You get responses directly, but you cannot add a profile picture to the alias.
  • Send-from in Juicebox: a hiring manager can grant permission and you send from their address. Responses go to their inbox.
  • Use "not interested" reasons to track pain points.

Things to test

  • Short and direct subject lines against longer, aspirational ones
  • Different message lengths, since longer messages sometimes drive more interest
  • Candidate-focused and personalized copy against mission-focused copy
  • Different media links to see which gets more clicks
  • InMail, email, and connection requests

Sample Claude prompt

We're hiring for a [role] at CodePath. Please create a three-part email sequence which I can send to prospective candidates who are currently at a senior, staff or principal level. Align the voice with our mission, vision, and external talking points, which are uploaded to your configuration. Make the messaging mission-oriented and exciting, and do not hallucinate. Ask any clarifying questions before you begin. Avoid using em dashes and refer to the External Talking Points guidelines for terminology guidance.

When in doubt, be concise, human, and inspired. You are inviting people to help reimagine the future of tech education.
